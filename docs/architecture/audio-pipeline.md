# Audio pipelines

## Dictation (Feature 001)

AVAudioEngine captures the microphone. A nonblocking audio callback feeds a bounded queue; normalization and inference run away from the callback and main actor. The implementation plan sets queue, chunk and duration limits. Overflow must stop capture with a visible recoverable error, never silently drop speech. Multilingual Parakeet is initially a batch/chunk adapter, not assumed to be a multilingual streaming API.

Default audio is ephemeral. Bounded chunks may spill to app-private temporary files; cleanup runs on every exit and at startup. No audio history or backup. Every nonempty transcription is persisted in bounded local history before insertion, including partial results. Confirmed delivery or Dismiss recovery clears recovery status without changing completeness or deleting text. Only confirmed explicit Delete removes history; Copy changes neither text nor recovery status. An uncertain insertion must not trigger automatic retries that duplicate text.

Capture the target application and focused editable element before showing UI. Revalidate the target before insertion; never send text to a newly focused application. Secure fields are ineligible. Unsupported, closed or inaccessible targets leave completed text available for explicit copy. Clipboard modification is user initiated in Feature 001.

## Meetings (Feature 004)

Two independent sources feed two independent tracks. The microphone is a second `AVAudioEngine` input tap into a `MeetingSampleRing` (the existing C ring in meeting mode: 32 slots × 4,096 frames × up to 8 channels, preallocated, overflow dropped whole and counted, admission never latched). System audio is an audio-only ScreenCaptureKit `SCStream` (`capturesAudio`, `excludesCurrentProcessAudio`, 48 kHz, 2 channels, no screen output) into a second ring; no virtual audio device is involved. Each track has a serial `MeetingTrackWorker` that emits at most 32 blocks per 10 ms tick into one 4,096-frame buffer (retained samples or timeline-preserving overflow silence), encodes through one `AVAudioConverter` to AAC-LC (microphone mono 64 kbit/s, system ≤ 2 channels 96 kbit/s, 48 kHz) and appends ADTS frames synchronously to a `.part` file it owns; `fsync` and a segment heartbeat every 5 s. Heartbeat delivery is independent of draining, bounded to one in-flight callback and one newest pending snapshot. New durable recordings retain lost intervals as silence, including terminal loss on finalization; dropped-frame counts and warnings remain visible. Old shortened recordings are not rewritten. See [ADR 0018](../adr/0018-capture-progress-and-timeline.md). A failed write, sync, rename or encode latches the track and the ring keeps dropping and counting while the coordinator ends the meeting within its 250 ms poll. Every pause, stop, sleep, source failure and device change finalizes the open segment (flush, fsync, rename `.part` → `.aac`, fsync directory); a resume opens the next sequence. Dictation and meetings are mutually exclusive. The codec decision and its evidence are in [ADR 0015](../adr/0015-adts-aac-segments.md).

## Meeting transcription (Feature 005)

Each recording worker can tee its PCM block into a separate `MeetingAnalysisTap` before encoding. The tap has a 32-slot × 4,096-frame ring, drops and counts overflow, and never makes recording wait for inference. A nil tap preserves the Feature 004 path. `AnalysisStreamMixer` channel-averages and converts each track to mono 16 kHz, stages at most 16,000 samples per track, and averages paired samples at half gain. A lone track plays at unity gain; the mixer waits up to the 8,000-sample staging threshold for its peer. Tap overflow advances the timeline and creates a gap.

The live path uses one 480,000-sample queue and one in-flight 96,000-sample (six-second) window. Lag above six seconds is catching up; above ten seconds it drops oldest whole windows and persists `backpressure` gaps. At capacity it suspends admission until occupancy falls to ten seconds. Pending PCM holes are bounded to 64 merged ranges. The two-window assembler, segmenter and pass-specific vocabulary normalizer produce provisional text in batches of at most 50, with at most 200 unpersisted segments. Failures stop transcription while recording continues.

`MeetingFinalizer` reads durable AAC tracks through `AVAudioFile`, at most 4,096 frames per track per read, and uses the same mixer with 239,360-sample recognition windows. It writes no derived audio files. Segment batches and progress commit together; a matching final pass resumes from persisted progress. Completion replaces provisional text and removes live gaps. Retry, Transcribe and automatic finalization share this path. Source files remain owned by recording and recovery.

See the [analysis contract](../../specs/005-live-meeting-transcription/contracts/live-analysis.md) for bounds and gap semantics. Real-model throughput was measured in Phase 2; live latency, long-run memory, production finalization and hardware recovery acceptance remain pending.

## Speaker diarization (Feature 007)

`MeetingDiarizer` runs after a meeting's transcript is `final`. It walks the same finalizer work list as transcription (pages of 100 stretches, the transcript's stretch bases) but decodes one track at a time through `AnalysisStreamMixer(decoding:)`, 4,096 frames per read, into one reusable 9,600,000-sample (10-minute) window buffer. The system track goes first, then the microphone; the microphone is diarized with `numSpeakers = 1` unless the meeting is marked in-room. It writes no derived audio.

Each full window and each stretch tail is diarized, reconciled against the run's clusters by centroid similarity (`WindowClusterReconciler`) and persisted before the next window is decoded. Turn times are converted to the recorded timeline as `stretch base + window offset + seconds × 1000` and clipped to the window and to the transcript's stretch length, so turns and transcript segments share one clock. After the lease is finished, alignment pages the final segments 200 at a time and reads only the turns overlapping each page's time span; `SpeakerAligner` labels a segment `speaker`, `unknown` or `ambiguous` from coverage alone. Text, notes and audio files are only read. See the [pipeline contract](../../specs/007-speaker-diarization/contracts/diarization-pipeline.md).
